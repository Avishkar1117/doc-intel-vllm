# Phase 8: SROIE - self-hosted vs Azure Document Intelligence (corrected, D-038)

Supersedes `phase8_writeup.md` (retained for reference). No new benchmarks were executed: both capture artifacts (`phase8_sroie_capture.jsonl` and `phase8_sroie_di_capture.jsonl`) remain identical, requiring no GPU time or additional DI API calls. Revisions apply strictly to the scoring methodology across two areas, identified during manual evaluation of the raw outputs.

## What changed, and why it matters

1. **Dates no longer penalize appended timestamps:** The model frequently produces outputs such as `15/01/2019 11:05:16 AM` where the ground truth records simply `15/01/2019`. The previous parser could not reconcile this format, falsely flagging valid calendar dates as mismatches. Stripping trailing timestamps across both targets and predictions confirmed that 59 of the model's 71 date misses were actually correct dates. The remaining 12 represent true classification errors (such as `26-03-19` vs. `26-03-18`, or missing year digits). Consequently, self-hosted date F1 improved from 0.794 to 0.965.
2. **DI's 21 "no Total" entries comprised three distinct root causes:** 15 cases reflected an omitted `Total` key in the response payload. 4 cases involved DI returning unexpected negative sums (-45.0, -35.0, -15.0, and -55.0 against printed totals of 408.45, 59.35, 47.15, and 83.55); these were originally logged as empty responses because the `SroieReceipt.total` validator enforced non-negative values. These are now explicitly scored as `total` classification failures. The remaining 2 samples contained negative values in the source ground truth itself (which DI matched), leading to both being removed from the test set for both systems.

**Net effect on baseline metrics:** The prior tie at 0.871 was an artifact of the rigid date parser. With the normalization in place, the self-hosted deployment leads overall at 0.914 versus 0.868. This performance advantage is driven primarily by `company` (+0.11) and `address` (+0.20), whereas DI retains an edge on `total` (+0.10) and `date` (+0.02).

## Findings

- **Cost and coverage remain the clear differentiators:** At $0.91 vs. $10.00 per 1,000 documents, self-hosting yields an 11x cost advantage. The local model provided complete predictions across all inputs, whereas DI returned no total on 15 of 347 receipts (4.3%) and hallucinated negative figures on another 4 (1.2%).
- **`total` extraction remains the self-hosted pipeline's bottleneck:** Out of 345 evaluations, the local model missed 60 totals. 48 predictions exceeded the ground-truth value (such as reading 200.0 instead of 169.8, or 102.0 instead of 72.0), while 12 fell below it (such as 6.0 instead of 6.35). Misidentifying subtotals or tendered cash as the final amount aligns with the upward skew, though validating this mechanism requires further qualitative inspection.
- **Single-request latency is effectively identical:** p50 performance tracks within standard network variance (3,893 ms vs. 3,773 ms).
- **Data privacy and deployment considerations:** For environments requiring strict document isolation within internal infrastructure, the self-hosted solution provides higher overall extraction accuracy, complete record coverage, and significantly lower operating costs. Conversely, for pipelines solely focused on isolating the final balance, DI's typed total extraction offers more consistent boundary identification.

## Caveats

- Evaluated as a single pass per receipt using default generation parameters (temperature 0.7) without multi-sample averaging. While a 4.6-point margin sits outside normal sampling variance, it lacks a formal confidence interval.
- The 4 invalid negative predictions from DI did not retain metadata for vendor, address, or date fields (due to capture validation failing before serialization). As a result, they penalize DI's `total` score without factoring into the other field tallies.
- DI's F1 metrics do not account for the 15 unreturned receipts, slightly boosting its individual field averages; coverage counts are provided to balance this comparison.
- Heuristics for the address overlap threshold (0.7) and the timestamp stripping pattern were calibrated against this dataset directly, rather than an unseen validation split.
- Analysis evaluates a single model architecture (4B reduced-res). Cost estimates apply Azure's standard S0 tier against measured local execution time at $0.000222 per GPU-second.
